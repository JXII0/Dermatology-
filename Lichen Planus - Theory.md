# 🟦 Lichen Planus - Theory

← [[Lichen Planus]] · Practical: [[Lichen Planus management]] · Test: [[Lichen Planus - Exam]]

**Level:** #theory #viva #papulosquamous

**Book pages:** 143 to 147

**Exam echo:** R2 Q34. R1 Q22, Q35.

## §1 Definition, cause and who gets it

> [!info] Definition
> An **idiopathic inflammatory disease of the skin and mucous membranes**. It is a **T-cell-mediated autoimmune** disease.

- **Triggers:** infection (**hepatitis C**), vaccine (**influenza**), drugs (**ACE inhibitors, thiazide diuretics, antimalarials**)
- **Associations:** alopecia areata, vitiligo, hepatitis C
- **Age:** two-thirds of patients start between **30 and 60**
- **Sex and race:** no predilection
- ➕ The T cells attack the basal cells of the epidermis. That is why the biopsy shows basal damage (§3)

## §2 Clinical picture

### The primary lesion

| Feature | What you see |
| --- | --- |
| Size and shape | **Small, polygonal** |
| Color | **Violaceous** (violet) |
| Top | **Flat-topped**; occasionally umbilicated |
| Surface | Slightly **shiny or transparent** |
| Fine lines | A net of white lines: **Wickham striae** (from focal hypergranulosis) |
| Spread | Scattered, or clustered and joined into **plaques** |
| After healing | **Dark marks** (post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation) |
| Koebner | May be seen |
| Symptom | **Itch**, of varying degrees |

> [!tip] Commonest type
> The commonest type of lichen planus is the **papular** type.

### Where it appears

| Site | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Skin | Flexor wrists and forearms, dorsal hands, shins, presacral area |
| Mucous membranes | Affected in **more than half** of patients, especially the mouth. May be the **only** site |
| Genitals | **Glans penis** in men (usually annular), **vulva** in women |
| Nails | May be affected (see §5) |
| Scalp | May be affected (see §5) |

## §3 Histology (the biopsy)

| Finding | What it is | What it explains |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hyperkeratosis | Thick horny layer | |
| **Focal hypergranulosis** | Patchy thickening of the granular layer | **Wickham striae** |
| Irregular acanthosis | Uneven thickening of the prickle layer (called saw-tooth in exam options ➕) | |
| **Vacuolar degeneration of the basal layer** | Damage to the basal cells | **Pigment incontinence** (dark marks) and, if severe, **separation and fluid collection** (bullous type) |
| **Band-like lymphocytic infiltrate** at the dermo-epidermal junction | The lichenoid pattern | The immune attack |
| **Civatte (colloid) bodies** | Degenerated keratinocytes | The hallmark |

> [!warning] Pitfall
> **Munro microabscesses** and **parakeratosis** belong to psoriasis. **Pautrier microabscesses** belong to mycosis fungoides. **Saw-tooth ridges** and **Civatte bodies** belong to lichen planus. See [[Psoriasis - Theory]].

## §4 The eight types

| Type | What it is | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Papular** | Small flat-topped violet papules | The commonest type |
| **Hypertrophic** | Warty lesions, mostly on the lower limbs and around the ankles (the shin); lichenification that does not resolve | **Worst type.** Complications: keratoacanthoma, malignant change, metastatic squamous cell carcinoma |
| **Atrophic** | Thin, shrunken lesions ➕ | |
| **Ulcerative** | Ulcerating lesions | **Worst type.** Risk of malignant change |
| **Follicular** (lichen planopilaris) | Violet, scaly, itchy follicular and perifollicular papules on the scalp | **Worst type.** Progresses to atrophic scarring alopecia |
| **Annular** | Ring-shaped lesions, commonest on the **glans penis** | Treated with a topical steroid |
| **Linear or zosteriform** | Lesions in a line or along a dermatome | |
| **Bullous** | Basal damage lets fluid collect and blisters form | |
| **Actinic** | Related to sun exposure | |

> [!danger] The three worst types
> **Hypertrophic**, **ulcerative** and **lichen planopilaris**. The first two can turn into squamous cell carcinoma. The third scars the scalp for good.

## §5 Special sites

### Mouth (oral lichen planus)

- May be **erosive** or **non-erosive**
- Commonest site: the **buccal mucosa**. Lesions are often **bilateral and symmetric**
- The commonest and most characteristic form is the **linear reticular** pattern (white lace). It is **usually asymptomatic**
- **Erosive** and **bullous** forms give symptoms from mild discomfort to severe pain
- **Ulcerated** oral lesions may have a higher incidence of **malignant change in men**

**The six oral presentations:**
1. Reticular (asymptomatic)
2. Papular
3. Plaque
4. Bullous
5. Erosive or ulcerative
6. Atrophic

### Nails

- Affected in about **10%** of patients, usually **several nails**
- **Pterygium unguis** is the **most specific sign**: the skin of the dorsal nail fold sticks to the nail bed and partly destroys the nail
- Also: longitudinal ridging, fissuring, yellow discoloration, onycholysis, subungual hyperkeratosis
- **Melanonychia** can also occur

### Scalp (lichen planopilaris)

- Skin lesions may be joined by **follicular and perifollicular** lesions on the scalp: violet, scaly, itchy papules
- These can progress to **atrophic cicatricial (scarring) alopecia**: **lichen planopilaris**

### Genitals

- **Glans penis** (usually annular) and **vulva**

### Hypertrophic lesions

- Warty lesions on the lower limbs, around the ankles
- Complications: **keratoacanthoma**, malignant change, metastatic squamous cell carcinoma

## §6 Course, prognosis and complications

**Prognosis**
- Spontaneous healing **may** occur within **1 year**
- It heals with **post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation**. The cause is **melanin pigmentary incontinence**: melanin collects in the **upper dermis**
- **Relapses** might occur

**Complications**
- **Malignant change** in the ulcerative and hypertrophic types (squamous cell carcinoma)
- **Scarring alopecia** in lichen planopilaris
- Side effects of **prolonged corticosteroid**
- **Residual hyperpigmentation**, a common marker after the disease has subsided

## §7 Tell it apart (the book’s differential)

| Looks like | How to tell it apart |
| --- | --- |
| Guttate psoriasis | Small raindrop papules with fine scale, often after a sore throat; no Wickham striae and no white lace in the mouth |
| Plaque psoriasis | Thick silvery scale, extensor sites, pitted nails |
| Secondary syphilis | Coppery lesions including palms and soles, not itchy; serology |
| Tinea corporis | Ring with an active scaly edge; KOH shows hyphae |
| Warts | Rough, wart-like surface; hypertrophic lichen planus can look warty too |
| Drug eruption | Starts after a new drug |

## §8 Connections

- **Looks like:** [[Psoriasis]], [[Secondary Syphilis]], [[Tinea Corporis]], warts, drug eruption
- **Same sign:** [[Koebner phenomenon]] with [[Psoriasis]]. Pterygium (lichen planus) against pitting ([[Psoriasis]], [[Alopecia]]). White lace in the mouth
- **Same test:** biopsy, as in [[Psoriasis]]. Terms in [[Skin Structure and Histology]]
- **Triggered by:** hepatitis C, influenza vaccine, ACE inhibitors, thiazides, antimalarials
- **Can lead to:** scarring alopecia, squamous cell carcinoma, dark marks
- **Treated with the same things as:** [[Psoriasis]]. See [[Lichen Planus management]]

**Mnemonic ➕:** *Purple, Polygonal, Pruritic, Planar, Papules, Plaques.* And for the mouth, nails and scalp: *Lace, Pterygium, Planopilaris.*

**Take-home:** Learn lichen planus as lesion, sites, histology and worst types. The white lines in the mouth, the pterygium of the nail and the Civatte bodies on the slide are the same disease seen three ways.

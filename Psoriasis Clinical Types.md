**Common sites for all types:** scalp, elbows, knees and sacrum, but any area can be involved. Clinical findings often **overlap**; different types may need **different treatment**.

|                      |                                               |                                                                          |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Type                 | Where                                         | What it looks like                                                       |
| Plaque               | Limbs, trunk, scalp                           | Well-defined, raised, red plaques with silvery scale                     |
| Guttate              | Trunk, limbs, scalp                           | Many small discrete red-pink papules (raindrops) with fine scale         |
| Inverse (flexural)   | Armpits, groin, under the breasts, skin folds | Smooth, dry, red patches that often lack scale because the area is moist |
| Erythrodermic        | Entire body                                   | Widespread redness, then peeling                                         |
| Generalized pustular | Entire body                                   | Pustules on red skin                                                     |
| Localized pustular   | Palms and soles                               | Pustules on red plaques                                                  |


### 1.Plaque (80-90%)

Well-demarcated erythematous plaques with **silvery scale**, typically **symmetric and bilateral**. May show **Auspitz** and **Koebner**.  
**Severity split:** ~**80%** mild-moderate (localized or scattered, **<5% BSA**); ~**20%** moderate-severe (**>5% BSA** or crucial areas: hands, feet, face, scalp, genitals).

![[IMG_4435.jpeg]]

### 2.Guttate

**Acute** sudden onset raindrop-sized lesions on trunk and extremities. **Often preceded by streptococcal pharyngitis / URTI.** **Good prognosis.**
![[IMG_4423.jpeg]]

### 3.Erythrodermic: "the WORST type"

Almost the whole skin surface bright red, with **fever, chills, malaise** (+ **oedema**, **hypoalbuminaemia**). Hospitalization sometimes needed (like generalized pustular).
![[IMG_4422.jpeg]]

### 4.Pustular: sterile "neutrophils"

Psoriatic lesions with pustules. Sterile and full of neutrophils. 
**Often triggered by corticosteroid withdrawal.** 
**Generalized (the von Zumbusch variant)= potentially life-threatening → hospitalize + dermatologist.**
Can be localized (palms/soles) 
![[IMG_4428.jpeg]]

### 5 Flexural (inverse)

Erythematous plaques in **axilla, groin, inframammary** and other folds. **May lack scale (moisture).** Bilateral, symmetrical.
![[IMG_4429.jpeg]]


> [!example] 🧠 P-G-I-E-P-P( Mnemonic )
> **P**laque · **G**uttate · **I**nverse · **E**rythrodermic · **P**ustular · **P**almoplantar. Dangerous pair: **E**rythrodermic and generalized **P**ustular.

## B. Traps

> [!warning] Yellow means versicolor
> **Golden or bright yellow Wood’s light is pityriasis versicolor.** Coral red is erythrasma, green is tinea capitis, bluish white is favus, accentuated white is vitiligo.

> [!warning] KOH can be negative in an exam question
> R2 Q29 gives a **negative KOH** with a **golden-yellow Wood’s light**, and the answer is pityriasis versicolor. In real life, repeat the scraping from a scaly area ➕.

> [!warning] Pityriasis alba
> It is on the **cheeks of children**, ill-defined, atopic, and **not yellow** under the lamp. R2 Q28.

> [!warning] Vitiligo
> It has **no scale** and is **fully white**. Pityriasis versicolor has fine scale.

> [!warning] Terbinafine
> It is **active against dermatophytes only**. It does not cover the yeast, so it is not the answer for pityriasis versicolor. **Ketoconazole by mouth is hepatotoxic (not used).**

> [!warning] Season
> It becomes obvious **after summer**, because the surrounding skin tans and the pale patches do not.

> [!warning] Inverse
> **Inverse** pityriasis versicolor means the **flexures**.

## C. Case questions

Try to answer before opening the answer.

**Q1.** A 25-year-old man in summer has pale, finely scaly macules on his upper trunk and shoulders. Under Wood’s light they glow yellow. What is the most likely diagnosis?
A) Vitiligo B) Pityriasis alba C) Pityriasis versicolor D) Tinea corporis

> [!success]- Answer
> **C.** Fine branny scale on the upper trunk plus yellow fluorescence is pityriasis versicolor. Vitiligo has no scale, pityriasis alba is on a child’s cheeks, and tinea corporis forms rings.

**Q2.** What does KOH show in pityriasis versicolor?
A) Hyphae only B) Spaghetti and meatballs C) Pseudohyphae with budding yeast only D) Arthrospores in a hair shaft

> [!success]- Answer
> **B.** Hyphae and round yeast forms together: spaghetti and meatballs.

**Q3.** What is the causative organism?
A) *Trichophyton rubrum* B) *Candida albicans* C) *Malassezia furfur or globosa* D) *Microsporum canis*

> [!success]- Answer
> **C.** A lipophilic yeast.

**Q4.** Which factor promotes the change from the round yeast to the mycelial form?
A) High temperature and humidity B) Cold dry weather C) Very dry skin D) Reduced sweating

> [!success]- Answer
> **A.** Also oily skin, sweating, immunodeficiency, poor nutrition, pregnancy, steroids and oils.

**Q5.** What is usually the main complaint?
A) Severe itch B) Pain C) Cosmetic disfigurement D) Fever

> [!success]- Answer
> **C.** The disease is generally asymptomatic.

**Q6.** Which Wood’s light color points to erythrasma rather than versicolor?
A) Bright yellow B) Coral or deep red C) Brilliant green D) Bluish white

> [!success]- Answer
> **B.** Coral red is erythrasma. Yellow is pityriasis versicolor.

**Q7.** Why do the patches stay pale for weeks after the fungus has been treated?
A) The fungus is still active B) Reduced tanning and inhibited melanin production C) Scarring D) Drug effect

> [!success]- Answer
> **B.** The fungus inhibits melanin production, and the skin recovers slowly.

**Q8.** An 8-year-old atopic child has ill-defined, pale, finely scaly patches on both cheeks after a summer holiday. What is the diagnosis?
A) Vitiligo B) Pityriasis versicolor C) Pityriasis alba D) Tinea corporis

> [!success]- Answer
> **C.** Pityriasis alba. Versicolor is usually on the upper trunk and glows yellow.

**Q9.** Which topical group treats pityriasis versicolor?
A) Azoles B) Corticosteroids C) Retinoids D) Calcineurin inhibitors

> [!success]- Answer
> **A.** Topical azoles, as cream, powder or lotion.

**Q10.** Which oral antifungal is active against dermatophytes only, and so does not suit pityriasis versicolor?
A) Fluconazole B) Itraconazole C) Terbinafine D) Ketoconazole

> [!success]- Answer
> **C.** Terbinafine does not cover the yeast.

**Q11.** What is inverse pityriasis versicolor?
A) Involvement of the flexures B) Hyperpigmented lesions only C) Lesions on the palms D) Lesions only on the face

> [!success]- Answer
> **A.** Flexural involvement.

**Q12.** Which condition of **depigmentation** must be told apart from pityriasis versicolor?
A) Vitiligo B) Pityriasis rosea C) Seborrheic dermatitis D) Tinea corporis

> [!success]- Answer
> **A.** Vitiligo is fully depigmented, with no scale, and accentuated white under Wood’s light.

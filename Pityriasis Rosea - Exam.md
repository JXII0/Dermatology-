## B. Traps

> [!warning] Palms and soles
> They are **usually (but not always) spared**, unlike syphilis. If an exam question asks which site is **uncommonly involved**, the answer is **palms and soles**.

> [!warning] Guttate psoriasis
> Guttate psoriasis has **no marginal collarette**. Pityriasis rosea does.

> [!warning] Christmas tree
> The pattern follows the **lines of cleavage**, not the dermatomes.

> [!warning] Herald patch
> It occurs in **80%**, not in every patient. It can also be multiple or be the only sign.

> [!danger] Syphilis
> **Secondary syphilis** is the look-alike you must not miss. The book says to **obtain serology**.

> [!warning] Course
> It is **self-limited**, with remission in **6 to 8 weeks**. It needs reassurance, not strong treatment.

## C. Case questions

Try to answer before opening the answer.

**Q1.** A 17-year-old girl in spring has a 3-cm oval pink plaque with fine scale at its edge on her back. Ten days later many smaller oval pink scaly patches appear on her trunk and upper arms. Her palms and soles are normal. What is the most likely diagnosis?
A) Tinea corporis B) Guttate psoriasis C) Pityriasis rosea D) Secondary syphilis

> [!success]- Answer
> **C.** A herald patch with a collarette, followed 1 to 2 weeks later by smaller oval lesions on the trunk and proximal limbs, sparing the palms and soles.

**Q2.** Which areas are usually spared in pityriasis rosea?
A) Trunk B) Proximal arms C) Palms and soles D) Thighs

> [!success]- Answer
> **C.** The face, palms and soles are usually (but not always) spared. Syphilis involves the palms and soles.

**Q3.** In what proportion of patients is a herald patch present?
A) 20% B) 50% C) 80% D) 100%

> [!success]- Answer
> **C.** About 80%.

**Q4.** Which virus reactivation is linked with pityriasis rosea?
A) HHV-6 and HHV-7 B) HSV-1 C) HPV D) EBV

> [!success]- Answer
> **A.** HHV-7 and HHV-6.

**Q5.** Which feature separates guttate psoriasis from pityriasis rosea?
A) Guttate lesions are red B) Guttate lesions have no marginal collarette C) Guttate lesions are on the trunk D) Guttate lesions itch

> [!success]- Answer
> **B.** Guttate psoriasis lacks the marginal collarette.

**Q6.** Which test is needed to exclude the look-alike that must not be missed?
A) KOH B) Wood’s light C) Syphilis serology D) Patch test

> [!success]- Answer
> **C.** Syphilis serology, because of secondary syphilis.

**Q7.** What is the expected course?
A) Clears in 2 weeks B) Remission in 6 to 8 weeks C) Lasts 6 months D) Chronic relapsing

> [!success]- Answer
> **B.** Spontaneous remission in 6 to 8 weeks. Recurrence is uncommon.

**Q8.** What is the best management of a mild case?
A) Oral prednisolone B) Methotrexate C) Reassurance with symptomatic treatment D) Oral antibiotics

> [!success]- Answer
> **C.** Education and reassurance, with an antipruritic lotion or a low to medium topical steroid if there is itch.

**Q9.** What is pityriasis rosea irritata?
A) A herald patch on the face B) A vesicular or erythema multiforme-like form due to sweat or inadequate treatment C) A chronic form D) A form with palm involvement

> [!success]- Answer
> **B.** It is caused by irritation from sweat or inadequate treatment.

**Q10.** Natural sunlight helps pityriasis rosea only if started when?
A) In the first week B) In the third week C) After the rash clears D) Never

> [!success]- Answer
> **A.** In the first week of the eruption.

**Q11.** Which findings does pityriasis rosea share with psoriasis on the slide?
A) Munro microabscesses and Civatte bodies B) Parakeratosis and absent granular layer C) Hypergranulosis and Civatte bodies D) Spongiosis and acantholysis

> [!success]- Answer
> **B.** Both show parakeratosis and an absent granular layer, so the clinical picture decides.

**Q12.** At which age is pityriasis rosea most common?
A) Infants B) Adolescents C) The elderly D) Middle age

> [!success]- Answer
> **B.** The peak is in adolescence (10 to 35). It is rare in infants and old people.

4.1 Traps examiners love

• Oral steroids: never in psoriasis (rebound). Only exception: pregnant with pustular psoriasis. 

• Methotrexate is WEEKLY. Daily dosing errors are fatal. 

• Koebner is not Auspitz is not pathergy. 

• Nail pitting: psoriasis and alopecia areata. 

• Guttate vs pityriasis rosea: guttate follows strep, small papules, no herald patch. 

• Inverse psoriasis has no scale (moisture). 

• PsA is seronegative: DIP and dactylitis, worse after rest. 

• Oil-drop sign is the most specific nail sign. 

• Erythrodermic is "the worst"; generalized pustular is life-threatening. 

4.2 Questions and answers

Q1. 24-year-old, small salmon-pink scaly papules on trunk 2 weeks after a sore throat? 

Answer: Guttate psoriasis. Good prognosis.

Q2. 45-year-old: silvery plaques on elbows and knees, nail pitting and oil-drop, sausage finger, DIP pain easing with movement, BSA about 12%. Diagnosis and approach? 

Answer: Plaque psoriasis plus psoriatic arthritis. Systemic therapy indicated (severity, nails, PsA). Baseline labs, avoid systemic steroids, review his drugs.

Q3. Stopped prednisolone, now widespread pustules and fever? 

Answer: Generalized pustular psoriasis (von Zumbusch) after steroid withdrawal. Admit.

Q4. Histologic basis of Auspitz sign? 

Answer: Thin suprapapillary plates over dilated papillary capillaries.

Q5. Pregnant woman with moderate plaque psoriasis: best treatment level? 

Answer: Phototherapy. Methotrexate is teratogenic.

Q6. Liver / renal / lipids monitoring (book cue)? 

Answer: Methotrexate / cyclosporin / acitretin.

Q7. Most specific sign for nail psoriasis? 

Answer: Oil-drop sign.

Q8. X-ray signs of PsA? 

Answer: "Cup and saucer" and "pencil" appearance.

Q9. Which topical agent has no contraindication with UVB despite photosensitivity? 

Answer: Calcipotriene (vitamin D derivative).

Q10. Six drugs that worsen psoriasis? 

Answer: Lithium, interferon, beta blockers, ACE inhibitors, antimalarials, NSAIDs/aspirin (plus steroid withdrawal, sulfa).

4.3 Flashcards (Obsidian Spaced Repetition format: question ::: answer)

Onset peaks in psoriasis ::: 20-30 and 50-60 years

First-degree relative affected ::: about 30%

Epidermal turnover ::: 2-3 days (normal 28)

Th cells and cytokines ::: Th1/Th17; IL-17, 12, 23, 22

Auspitz sign cause ::: superficial vessel enlargement + thin suprapapillary ridges

Plaque type frequency ::: 80-90%

Mild-moderate disease cut-off (book) ::: <5% BSA

Guttate trigger ::: streptococcal pharyngitis / URTI

Pustular trigger ::: corticosteroid withdrawal

Generalized pustular psoriasis name ::: von Zumbusch

PsA frequency ::: about 30%

PsA most distinctive features ::: DIP arthritis, dactylitis

Nail involvement ::: fingers 50%, toes 35%

Most specific nail sign ::: oil-drop sign

DDx of nail pitting ::: psoriasis, alopecia areata

Histology key ::: parakeratosis, Munro microabscess, absent granular layer, test-tube acanthosis

Dermoscopy ::: white scale + red dotted (glomerular) vessels

First-line topical ::: high-potency steroid with or without calcipotriene

Phototherapy schedule ::: 20-25 sessions, 2-3 per week

Methotrexate dose (book) ::: 7.5-25 mg per week

Cyclosporin dose (book) ::: 2.5-5 mg/kg/day

Biologic targets ::: TNF-alpha, IL-12/23, IL-17

Drugs worsening psoriasis ::: LIBAN + S

Systemic option in pregnancy ::: phototherapy

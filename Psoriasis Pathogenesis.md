**Level:** #theory #viva #papulosquamous

**Book pages:** 132 to 133

**The idea in one line:** an overactive immune system makes skin cells divide far too fast, and the skin cannot keep up.

**Step by step:**

1. **Genes plus environment.** It is a complex genetic disease with many triggers. About 30% of patients have an affected first-degree relative.
2. **Immune activation.** Activated Th1 and Th17 T cells release cytokines: IL-17, IL-12, IL-23 and IL-22.
3. **Overgrowth.** These cytokines push the epidermal cells to divide, so the skin becomes thick and makes excess scale.
4. **Fast turnover.** Epidermal turnover drops from the normal 28 days to only 2 to 3 days. Cells have no time to mature, so they keep their nuclei (parakeratosis), the granular layer disappears, and scale piles up silvery and thick.
5. **The dermis joins in.** Capillaries become dilated and long, which gives the red color and explains the Auspitz sign.

**Why this matters for treatment:** most treatments either calm the T cells or block the cytokines. Biologics block TNF-alpha, IL-12 and 23, or IL-17. See [[Psoriasis Treatment]].

**Extra (not in your book):** IL-23 keeps Th17 cells active, and IL-17 acts on the skin cells themselves. That is why drugs aimed at these two work so well.


**Mnemonic:** _Fast Cells, Fast Scale._ A 2 to 3 day turnover instead of 28 days.

**Take-home:** Every feature of psoriasis, from the silvery scale to the bleeding spots, follows from skin cells being made too fast.

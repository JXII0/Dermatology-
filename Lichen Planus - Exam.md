## B. Traps

> [!warning] Histology
> **Civatte bodies and saw-tooth ridges are lichen planus. Munro microabscesses are psoriasis.** The exam puts them as options in the same question.

> [!warning] Nails
> **Pterygium is lichen planus. Pitting is psoriasis and alopecia areata.** Both are nail signs, so learn them as a pair.

> [!warning] Site
> **A ring-shaped lesion on the glans penis means annular lichen planus.**

> [!warning] Wickham striae
> They come from **focal hypergranulosis**, not from parakeratosis or acanthosis.

> [!warning] Dark marks
> They come from **pigment incontinence** (melanin in the upper dermis), not from extra melanocytes.

> [!warning] Mouth
> The commonest form is the **reticular** pattern, which is **painless**. The painful ones are erosive and bullous.

> [!danger] Worst types
> **Hypertrophic, ulcerative and lichen planopilaris.** The first two carry a cancer risk. The last one scars the scalp.

## C. Case questions

Try to answer before opening the answer.

**Q1.** A 45-year-old man has itchy flat-topped violet polygonal papules on both flexor wrists and white lace-like lines on the buccal mucosa. What is the most likely diagnosis?
A) Psoriasis B) Lichen planus C) Pityriasis rosea D) Secondary syphilis

> [!success]- Answer
> **B.** Violet flat-topped itchy papules on the flexor wrists plus white lace lines in the mouth is lichen planus.

**Q2.** A biopsy shows degenerated keratinocytes in the lower epidermis. What are they called?
A) Munro microabscesses B) Civatte bodies C) Pautrier microabscesses D) Spongiotic vesicles

> [!success]- Answer
> **B.** Civatte (colloid) bodies are degenerated keratinocytes, the hallmark of lichen planus. Munro microabscesses are psoriasis, Pautrier microabscesses are mycosis fungoides, and spongiosis is eczema.

**Q3.** What produces Wickham striae?
A) Parakeratosis B) Focal hypergranulosis C) Acantholysis D) Spongiosis

> [!success]- Answer
> **B.** Focal thickening of the granular layer gives the white net.

**Q4.** A man has a ring-shaped violet lesion on the glans penis. Which type is this?
A) Hypertrophic B) Annular C) Follicular D) Actinic

> [!success]- Answer
> **B.** The annular type is commonest on the glans, and topical steroid is usually enough.

**Q5.** Which nail change is most specific for lichen planus?
A) Pitting B) Oil drop sign C) Pterygium D) Beau lines

> [!success]- Answer
> **C.** Pterygium unguis. The oil drop sign is the most specific nail sign in psoriasis.

**Q6.** A man has had a warty, thickened, lichenified plaque on the shin for years that never clears. What is the best treatment, and what must you watch for?
A) Emollients alone B) Intralesional corticosteroid, and watch for squamous cell carcinoma C) Oral antifungal D) Phototherapy only

> [!success]- Answer
> **B.** Hypertrophic lichen planus is treated with intralesional steroid, and it carries a risk of keratoacanthoma and squamous cell carcinoma.

**Q7.** A patient has violet, scaly, itchy follicular papules on the scalp, and patches of permanent hair loss. Which route of corticosteroid is advised?
A) Topical B) Intralesional C) Systemic D) None

> [!success]- Answer
> **C.** This is lichen planopilaris, one of the worst types, treated with systemic steroid. It causes scarring alopecia.

**Q8.** Which infection is linked with lichen planus?
A) Hepatitis C B) Streptococcal throat infection C) Malaria D) Tuberculosis

> [!success]- Answer
> **A.** Hepatitis C. A strep throat is the trigger for guttate psoriasis, so do not mix them.

**Q9.** Why does lichen planus leave dark marks after healing?
A) More melanocytes B) Pigment incontinence C) Hemosiderin deposits D) Drug deposits

> [!success]- Answer
> **B.** Damage to the basal layer lets melanin fall into the upper dermis.

**Q10.** What is the commonest pattern of oral lichen planus?
A) Erosive B) Bullous C) Linear reticular D) Atrophic

> [!success]- Answer
> **C.** The linear reticular pattern, usually painless.

**Q11.** Which of these is NOT one of the three worst clinical types?
A) Hypertrophic B) Ulcerative C) Follicular (lichen planopilaris) D) Annular

> [!success]- Answer
> **D.** Annular. The three worst are hypertrophic, ulcerative and lichen planopilaris.

**Q12.** A patient has severe mouth lichen planus that has not responded to the usual treatment. Which route of corticosteroid is advised?
A) Topical only B) Intralesional C) Systemic D) None

> [!success]- Answer
> **C.** Systemic. Intralesional steroid is for hypertrophic, ulcerative and erosive mouth lesions. Severe mouth disease not responding to usual treatment gets systemic treatment.

# 🟥 Psoriasis - Exam
← [[Psoriasis]]

## Traps examiners love
> [!danger] 1. Oral steroids
> Never in psoriasis (rebound). Only exception: **pregnant with pustular psoriasis**.
> [!danger] 2. Methotrexate is **weekly**
> Daily dosing errors are fatal. See [[Methotrexate]].
> [!danger] 3. Koebner ≠ Auspitz ≠ pathergy
> Koebner = new lesions on trauma. Auspitz = bleeding when scale removed. **Pathergy** = pustule after needle prick (Behçet). See [[Koebner phenomenon]].
> [!danger] 4. Nail pitting
> Psoriasis **and** alopecia areata.
> [!danger] 5. Guttate vs pityriasis rosea
> Guttate: strep, small papules, no herald patch. See [[Papulosquamous DDx]].
> [!danger] 6. Inverse has **no scale** (moisture).
> [!danger] 7. PsA is **seronegative**: DIP + dactylitis, worse after rest.
> [!danger] 8. Oil-drop sign = most specific nail sign.
> [!danger] 9. Erythrodermic = "the worst"; generalized pustular = life-threatening.

## Questions (click to reveal)
> [!question]- Q1. 24-year-old, small salmon-pink scaly papules on trunk 2 weeks after sore throat?
> > [!success] Guttate psoriasis. Good prognosis.

> [!question]- Q2. 45-year-old: silvery plaques on elbows/knees, nail pitting + oil-drop, sausage finger, DIP pain easing with movement, BSA ~12%. Diagnosis and approach?
> > [!success] Plaque psoriasis + PsA. Systemic therapy indicated (severity, nails, PsA). Baseline labs, avoid systemic steroids, review his drugs.

> [!question]- Q3. Stopped prednisolone, now widespread pustules and fever?
> > [!success] Generalized pustular psoriasis (von Zumbusch) after steroid withdrawal. Admit.

> [!question]- Q4. Histologic basis of Auspitz sign?
> > [!success] Thin suprapapillary plates over dilated papillary capillaries.

> [!question]- Q5. Pregnant woman, moderate plaque psoriasis: best treatment level?
> > [!success] Phototherapy. Methotrexate is teratogenic.

> [!question]- Q6. Liver / renal / lipids monitoring (book cue)?
> > [!success] Methotrexate / cyclosporin / acitretin.

> [!question]- Q7. Which sign is most specific for nail psoriasis?
> > [!success] Oil-drop sign.

> [!question]- Q8. Name the X-ray signs of PsA.
> > [!success] "Cup and saucer" and "pencil" appearance.

> [!question]- Q9. Which topical agent has no contraindication with UVB despite photosensitivity?
> > [!success] Calcipotriene (vitamin D derivative).

> [!question]- Q10. Name 6 drugs that worsen psoriasis.
> > [!success] Lithium, interferon, β-blockers, ACE inhibitors, antimalarials, NSAIDs/aspirin (+ steroid withdrawal, sulfa).

## Flashcards (Spaced Repetition plugin)
Onset peaks in psoriasis ::: 20-30 and 50-60 years
First-degree relative affected ::: about 30%
Epidermal turnover ::: 2-3 days (normal 28)
Th cells and cytokines ::: Th1/Th17; IL-17, 12, 23, 22
Auspitz sign cause ::: superficial vessel enlargement + thin suprapapillary ridges
Plaque type frequency ::: 80-90%
Mild-moderate disease cut-off (book) ::: <5% BSA
Guttate trigger ::: streptococcal pharyngitis / URTI
Pustular trigger ::: corticosteroid withdrawal
Generalized pustular psoriasis name ::: von Zumbusch
PsA frequency ::: ~30%
PsA most distinctive features ::: DIP arthritis, dactylitis
Nail involvement ::: fingers 50%, toes 35%
Most specific nail sign ::: oil-drop sign
DDx of nail pitting ::: psoriasis, alopecia areata
Histology key ::: parakeratosis, Munro microabscess, absent granular layer, test-tube acanthosis
Dermoscopy ::: white scale + red dotted (glomerular) vessels
First-line topical ::: high-potency steroid ± calcipotriene
Phototherapy schedule ::: 20-25 sessions, 2-3 per week
Methotrexate dose (book) ::: 7.5-25 mg per week
Cyclosporin dose (book) ::: 2.5-5 mg/kg/day
Biologic targets ::: TNF-α, IL-12/23, IL-17
Drugs worsening psoriasis ::: LIBAN + S
Systemic option in pregnancy ::: phototherapy

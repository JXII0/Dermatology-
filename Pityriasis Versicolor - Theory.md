# 🟦 Pityriasis Versicolor - Theory

← [[Pityriasis Versicolor]] · Practical: [[Pityriasis Versicolor management]] · Test: [[Pityriasis Versicolor - Exam]]

**Level:** #theory #viva #papulosquamous

**Book pages:** 81 to 82 (and 86 to 87 for treatment)

**Exam echo:** R2 Q7, Q8, Q29. Related: R2 Q28, R1 Q25, R1 Q39.

## §1 Definition, cause and triggers

> [!info] Definition
> A **lipophilic yeast** that normally lives on human skin in amounts too small to be seen on **KOH**. The disease appears when the **round yeast form changes into the mycelial form**.

| Feature | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Organism | ***Malassezia furfur*** (yeast) or ***Malassezia globosa*** |
| Age | Any age, but **common in adults** (after puberty) |
| The change | Round yeast form to mycelial form |

**Factors that promote the change** (book):
1. High ambient temperature and humidity
2. Oily skin
3. Excessive sweating
4. Immunodeficiency
5. Poor nutrition
6. Pregnancy
7. **Corticosteroid use**
8. Application of oils (organic oils such as olive oil)

## §2 Clinical picture

### The lesion

| Feature | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Shape | **Multiple oval to round macules and patches** |
| Scale | **Mild, fine, branny** |
| Pattern | May be **follicular**: pinpoint lesions around the hair follicles |
| Color | **Hyperpigmented or hypopigmented**. A transient **pink** color can be seen when there is mild inflammation |
| Symptoms | Usually **asymptomatic**. The main complaint is **cosmetic disfigurement** |
| After treatment | **Secondary (post-eruptive) hypopigmentation**: reduced tanning, and **inhibition of melanin production** by the fungus |

### Where it appears

| Site | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Common | **Seborrheic regions**: upper trunk (front and back) and shoulders, a **short jacket with short sleeves** pattern |
| Less common | Face (especially in children), scalp, antecubital fossae, under the breasts, groin |
| Flexural | Involvement of the flexures is called **inverse pityriasis versicolor** |

## §3 The tests: lamp, slide and culture

| Test | Result | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Wood’s light** | **Bright yellow** fluorescence | Used for screening |
| **KOH scraping** | Both **hyphae and round yeast** forms: **spaghetti and meatballs** | KOH 10 to 20% |
| Culture | Generally **not needed** | Sabouraud dextrose agar is used for other fungi |

> [!warning] Active or post-eruptive?
> A hypopigmented patch with **fine scale** is **active disease**. A patch **without scale** does **not** prove it is post-eruptive. Use the **Wood’s light**: yellow fluorescence means the fungus is still there.

**General diagnosis of superficial fungal infection** (book): history, clinical picture, then investigations: **Wood’s light** (screening), **scraping with KOH 10 to 20%** (hyphae, spores), and **culture** on Sabouraud dextrose agar.

## §4 Why the color changes ➕

- The fungus **inhibits melanin production** (book), so treated skin stays pale until tanning and melanin recover
- ➕ This is why lesions become obvious in summer, when the surrounding skin tans and the pale patches stay pale
- ➕ The hyperpigmented form is the more common one in darker or tanned skin

## §5 Differential diagnosis (the book’s eight)

| Looks like | How to tell it apart |
| --- | --- |
| **Pityriasis alba** | Atopic patients; **ill-defined scaly whitish** lesions in predisposed areas (cheeks of children); no yellow Wood’s light |
| **Other post-inflammatory hypopigmentation** | History of a previous rash |
| **Vitiligo** (depigmentation) | Fully white, sharp edge, **no scale**; **accentuated white** under Wood’s light |
| **Seborrheic dermatitis** | Greasy scale, red patches on the scalp, face and folds |
| **Pityriasis rosea** | Herald patch, collarette, Christmas tree |
| **Tinea corporis** | Ring with a raised active edge; KOH shows hyphae only |
| **Secondary syphilis** | Coppery lesions, palms and soles; serology |
| **Progressive macular hypomelanosis** | Pale macules on the trunk without scale ➕ |

## §6 Course ➕

- Chronic, and it **tends to recur**, especially in warm and humid seasons, or while the triggers persist
- No serious complications. The concern is cosmetic

## §7 Connections

- **Looks like:** [[Pityriasis Alba]], vitiligo, [[Seborrheic Dermatitis]], [[Pityriasis Rosea]], [[Tinea Corporis]], [[Secondary Syphilis]]
- **Same sign:** fine branny scale; seborrheic distribution
- **Same test:** Wood’s light and KOH. See [[Dermatology Investigations]]
- **Triggered by:** heat, humidity, sweating, oily skin, steroids, pregnancy, immunodeficiency, oils
- **Treated with the same things as:** [[Tinea Corporis]] and [[Seborrheic Dermatitis]]. See [[Pityriasis Versicolor management]]

**Mnemonic ➕:** *Hot, Humid, Oily, Sweaty, Steroid.* The triggers that make the yeast become mycelial.

**Take-home:** Learn it as a yeast that changes shape in warm, oily, sweaty skin. The lamp is yellow, the slide is spaghetti and meatballs, and the skin stays pale for a while after cure because the fungus inhibits melanin.
